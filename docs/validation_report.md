## Variance, Failure Modes, and Tradeoffs

- Qualified starts: low latency and straightforward counting, but exposure and attention can inflate the numerator. Positive association does not prevent policy-effect reversal.
- Viewing days: less runtime-dependent and capped by the window, but repeated short visits can still increase without return benefit; the alternative is not certified.
- Watch time: heavy-use households and long titles change raw minutes. Pooled 99th-percentile caps, runtime subsets, genre subsets, and shorter windows show sensitivity; each transformation has a distinct estimand.
- Completion among starters: descriptive household any-completion ratio. Selection occurs after assignment; bootstrap precision does not repair causal selection bias.
- Exposure: presentation changes impressions. ITT is the total assigned-policy effect. Per-impression results condition on a post-assignment quantity and are diagnostics only.

Household-bootstrap SDs and intervals quantify sampling variation for each run. Full sensitivity estimates, pooled caps, genre mix, exposure balance, and executable SQL queries are in `reports/latest.json`. All integrity checks must pass before interpretation.

## Recommendation

Reject qualified starts as a universal surrogate; viewing days also requires independent validation. No candidate is authorized to replace the target.

Use the directly observed return target for decisions. Reject a universal starts-only rule. Follow up with new independent, preregistered experiment populations and policy classes; evaluate practical launch decisions, guardrails, equivalence under null, and transportability. Learned composite proxies are deferred until this baseline is reproducible and must respect train/validation/held-out freezing.
